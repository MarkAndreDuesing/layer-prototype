# Persistent layers win; evaluation and players remain open

Checked 2026-11-09. Sources: the six notes in `research_notes/Layered loop open paths/`, plus `reports/Research gap and questions.md` and `research_notes/Layered methods deep dive/` (heavy_models_recheck, release_status_recheck). Where they disagree, the newer notes win. [U] means the claim comes from a snippet or our own inference and was not checked on a primary page.

The loop we want (text → coherent RGBA layers → per-layer re-prompt/move/resize → recomposite → story events) is now buildable on Kaggle's free 2× T4 from released parts. What is still missing is evidence, not mechanics. **Qwen-Image-2.1** generates, edits and extracts RGBA layers natively, and fits a T4 as a 3.9 GB GGUF with 4–8-step accelerators. Its catch is that its stock VAE produces NaNs in fp16 *exactly on transparent pixels*, so the texture-fix VAE is required. The FLUX-class layer models (RevealLayer, CLD, ART+, LayerDiffuse-Flux) all have legitimate weights, and the ART base LoRA now sits officially in Apache-2.0 `thuteam/CLD`. Our RevealLayer pure-noise output is most likely a RevealLayer-code fp16 bug rather than a base-FLUX overflow. For harmonisation, IC-Light v1 fbc (SD1.5) plus a procedural shadow layer is the T4-sized answer. For story events there is still no layered method, and the best design is "edit background, relight foregrounds, add effect layers". The new literature sharpens the research gaps rather than closing them. Multi-turn benchmarks (EdiVal, MTEdit, TailSteer) now quantify flat-editor drift precisely, but none includes a layered editor. **ImageDirector (UIST '26)** is the first novice study of object-level direct manipulation, but it has no persistent layers, no story loop and only three edits. Our contribution should therefore be a long-sequence layered-vs-flat drift study and a player study, with Qwen-Image-2.1 and the existing SDXL/QIL stack as the engine.

## Qwen-Image-2.1 covers three loop steps but needs the texture-fix VAE

The model's RGBA support is native. Its VAE takes 4-channel RGBA in and out, compressed to a 64-channel latent at 16× spatial reduction ([GitHub README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image-2.1)). Generation and editing share one diffusers pipeline. That pipeline converts every condition image to RGBA, composites alpha over white only for the Qwen3-VL encoder copy, has **no mask or bbox argument**, and takes its output aspect from the *last* image ([diffusers pipeline](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/diffusers/main/src/diffusers/pipelines/qwenimage21/pipeline_qwenimage21.py)). The only official prompt anywhere is the transparency wrapper "This is an RGBA image with transparency. … The image has alpha channel and the background is transparent." ([HF Space app.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/raw/main/app.py)). ComfyUI's official background-removal template uses "Remove the background, and output a PNG image." ([Comfy docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/image/qwen/qwen-image-2-1)). There is **no layer-decomposition mode** and no announced layered successor. Qwen-Image-Layered (Apache-2.0) remains the only Qwen decomposer ([Qwen-Image-Layered](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image-Layered)).

On quality, no independent RGBA benchmark exists. One provider guide reports near-binary alpha (a glass bottle is about 99% opaque inside its outline), pale fringes on dark backgrounds, and the need to say "no floor and no shadow" explicitly ([Runware](https://runware.ai/docs/models/alibaba-qwen-image-2-1-pro/guides/transparent-backgrounds)). That guide covers the Pro endpoint, which may differ from the open weights. The practical rule follows: use 2.1 for opaque props and characters, and leave glass, smoke and shadows to compositing.

The decisive T4 fact concerns the VAE. The original decoder **NaNs in fp16 mostly in fully transparent regions** (75 of 185 stress inputs; peak activation about 3.5M versus fp16's 65,504). The madebyollin texture-fix VAE cuts the peak to about 1.3k with zero NaNs and a slightly better rFID ([texture-fix VAE](https://huggingface.co/madebyollin/texture-fix-vae-for-qwen-image-2.1)). Community speeds bound the cost. An RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB with INT8 weights takes about 20 s per generation and about 60 s per edit at 1024², 25 steps, peaking near 15 GB ([HF disc #35](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/discussions/35)). Two reference images add about 4 GB of peak VRAM ([HF disc #32](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/discussions/32)). Unsloth GGUF quants run from Q4_K_S at 3.91 GB to F16 at 14.2 GB, and a Q4_K_XL Qwen3-VL encoder is available ([unsloth GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen-Image-2.1-GGUF)). Several accelerators exist: Turbo (8 steps), a LightX2V Lightning 4-step LoRA ([ComfyUI wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-10-13-qwen-image-2-1-lightning)), Lightning v2 RL, and PAI Fun-Acc. Comfy reports local edits are fine at 4–8 steps, while full-frame edits want about 25. One user saw an all-NaN text encoder even in bf16, suspected to be corrupt files ([Turbo disc #6](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo/discussions/6)), so hash-check the shards. The licence permits non-commercial "research or evaluation" use ([LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE)), which fits this prototype under a permissive reading.

**What to do.** Extend the queued `make_qwen21_rgba_trial.py` with four changes. First, swap in `AutoencoderKLQwenImage21` from the texture-fix repo. Second, assert that `prompt_embeds` contains no NaNs. Third, run Turbo or Lightning at 4–8 steps. Fourth, add two tests: re-prompt an existing RGBA layer as `image_1` with the wrapper plus instruction, and harmonise a rough composite with a blend instruction (untested in any source). Treat 2.1 as the per-layer re-prompt / new-prop / extraction engine. Keep QIL for the initial split, and do moves deterministically in PIL.

## FLUX layer models have clean weights and a likely code bug

Both reference implementations handle FLUX fp16 with nothing more than a ±65504 clamp after residual adds. Diffusers clips single-block image states and double-block text states ([transformer_flux.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/diffusers/main/src/diffusers/models/transformers/transformer_flux.py)). ComfyUI applies `nan_to_num` in the same places plus once on the final image ([ComfyUI layers.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/comfyanonymous/ComfyUI/master/comfy/ldm/flux/layers.py)). Our `fp16_mixed` (fp32 residual, norms and gates) is stricter than either. That makes RevealLayer's own code the prime suspect. Its official pipeline is bf16-only. It builds its ROI mask with `-1e9` in the hidden dtype, which becomes −inf in fp16. It keeps `layer_pe` as an fp32 parameter added to fp16 states, leaves the refiner `delta` unguarded, defines a `Clamp` module that is never used, and ships a **vendored `diffusers/` copy** that our patch may not reach ([custom_model_mmdit.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/360CVGroup/RevealLayer/main/models/custom_model_mmdit.py); [repo tree](https://api.github.com/repos/360CVGroup/RevealLayer/git/trees/main?recursive=1)). The new ComfyUI-TBG-RevealLayer port officially accepts FP16 FLUX.1-dev but not quantized transformers ([TBG port](https://github.com/Ltamann/ComfyUI-TBG-RevealLayer)).

Weight provenance is now settled enough to proceed. A SHA-256 comparison (coordinator, not re-checked) shows CLD's `pre_trained_LoRA` is byte-identical to the ART base LoRA in the unofficial mirror. That puts the ART base officially on HF under Apache-2.0 ([thuteam/CLD](https://huggingface.co/thuteam/CLD/blob/main/README.md)). CLD's `prism_ft_LoRA` differs from the mirror's ART+ 100k LoRA. It is probably the non-100k ART+ PrismLayersPro checkpoint [U]. CLD itself needs an input image plus boxes ([arXiv 2511.16249](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16249v2)). ART+ is the only one of the four that does **text + box layout → layers** with no image, using a JSON of a style caption plus boxes ([PrismLayers](https://github.com/redredsheep/PrismLayers)), and it beats ART in roughly 57–60% of pairwise votes ([arXiv 2505.22523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22523v1)). LayerDiffuse-Flux is a LoRA plus TransparentVAE under AFL-3.0 with almost no community uptake ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/RedAIGC/Flux-version-LayerDiffuse)). Nobody has published T4 s/it numbers for FLUX. A free-Colab T4 notebook confirms FLUX runs with fp8 or GGUF Q4/Q5 ([comfyui_colab_Flux](https://github.com/sayantan-2/comfyui_colab_Flux)). Our own 69 s for 12 steps at 512 px is the only T4 datapoint. No FLUX.2, Kontext or Qwen ports of these four models exist.

**What to do.** First, run a plain-FLUX control through our GGUF+`fp16_mixed` loader with RevealLayer's LoRA, `layer_pe` and refiner disabled. Second, if that image is clean, patch RevealLayer in four places: build the mask in fp32 or clamp it to −1e4; cast `layer_pe` to the hidden dtype; clamp the refiner `delta`; and make sure the patch targets the vendored module classes. Third, port ART+ next. Use `thuteam/CLD`'s LoRAs as the source and Ollama-generated boxes at 512². ART+ is the only FLUX path that matches "text prompt → layers".

## Harmonisation fits in seconds with IC-Light and shadow layers

IC-Light v1 `fbc` (SD1.5, Apache-2.0) is the strongest background-conditioned relighter that fits a T4. It widens the UNet's `conv_in` to 12 channels and concatenates foreground and background latents onto the noise ([gradio_demo_bg.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lllyasviel/IC-Light/main/gradio_demo_bg.py)). Grey-fill the transparent area before encoding, or the background darkens ([RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/ComfyUI-IC-Light/ICLightConditioning)). IC-Light v2 (FLUX) never released weights ([discussion #98](https://github.com/lllyasviel/IC-Light/discussions/98)). LBM relighting is a one-step SDXL fine-tune but CC BY-NC, and its card example runs in bf16 ([LBM card](https://huggingface.co/jasperai/LBM_relighting)). The Qwen-Edit-2509 Relight and Fusion LoRAs ([Fusion](https://huggingface.co/dx8152/Qwen-Image-Edit-2509-Fusion)) and libcom's Kontext harmonisation LoRAs ([libcom](https://github.com/bcmi/libcom)) are 12–20B models and belong in an offline polish step.

No released model outputs a shadow *matte* for still images. GPSDiffusion (SD1.5 and SDXL, in libcom) composites shadows directly, and its README warns results are unstable, so generate several and pick one ([GPSDiffusion](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Zhao_Shadow_Generation_Using_Diffusion_Model_with_Geometry_Prior_CVPR_2025_paper.html)). EasyOmnimatte synthesises shadow mattes by squashing, shearing and blurring the foreground alpha ([supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Hu_EasyOmnimatte_Taming_Pretrained_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)), which gives a procedural shadow layer that moves with its object. DreamLayer's ablation shows that, without harmonisation, an object "floats" with no shadow ([arXiv 2503.12838](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12838v1)). On identity, no paper compares pinning, KV sharing, IP-Adapter and paste-back head to head. RegionDrag's mutual self-attention (reusing K/V from the source path) is the best-evidenced training-free choice ([arXiv 2407.18247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.18247)). Measure identity with DINO subject consistency plus background CLIP/LPIPS, as in [arXiv 2507.23300](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23300).

ImageDirector adds a design warning. Its experts rated text-only regeneration **no worse** on lighting and cohesion, because regenerating the whole scene incidentally relights the object. Its users also preferred seeing the raw moved proxy immediately over waiting for harmonisation ([ImageDirector, UIST '26](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830709)).

**What to do.** Extend `relocate()` v5 in five steps. First, pin only the eroded interior. Second, add source-pass K/V sharing. Third, run IC-Light fbc at 0.4–0.6 strength on GPU 1 against the object-free background. Fourth, apply a frequency-split paste-back and the original alpha. Fifth, emit a procedural shadow RGBA layer between background and object. In the UI, show the plain move instantly and swap in the harmonised layer when it is ready.

## Story events have no layered method, so we design one

No layered paper applies weather, time-of-day or fire edits consistently across layers. The nearest systems are flat or 3D. WeatherEdit separates background editing from particles on a Gaussian-splat scene ([GitHub](https://github.com/Jumponthemoon/WeatherEdit)). SceneCrafter does weather and sun angle for driving scenes, with no code ([arXiv 2506.19488](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19488v1)). LightIt conditions on shading maps, also with no code ([project](https://peter-kocsis.github.io/LightIt)). For "same edit on every layer", StyleAligned's shared attention on SDXL is the drop-in mechanism (Apache-2.0, archived, includes a ControlNet-Depth notebook) ([GitHub](https://github.com/google/style-aligned)). DreamLayer's layer-shared self-attention is the principled version, but no editing code has been released ([arXiv 2503.12838](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12838v1)). Rerender needs 24 GB and is out ([GitHub](https://github.com/williamyang1991/Rerender_A_Video)). LayerDiffuse can already generate a foreground conditioned on an existing background, including semi-transparent fire ([arXiv 2402.17113](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17113v3)). That makes it the natural generator for fog, fire and glow layers. Game engines render rain and snow as particles and shaders ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.shadergraph@14.0/manual/Shader-Graph-Sample-Production-Ready-Weather.html)). Indie visual-novel developers lock the layout and pre-generate one variant per state ([itch.io](https://itch.io/blog/1145381/solved-how-to-keep-ai-backgrounds-geometrically-consistent-day-vs-night)).

No source compares strategies quantitatively, which turns the comparison into a contribution. **What to do.** Implement an event router with four arms and compare them on the same events (rainy night, forest fire, dusk). The router is an Ollama call that maps an event to a per-layer action of keep, relight, re-prompt or add effect layer. The four arms are: (a) edit the composite with 2.1 or SDXL, then re-split with QIL; (b) edit every RGBA layer with the same 2.1 instruction; (c) edit only the background, then relight foregrounds with IC-Light fbc and reapply alpha; (d) arm (c) plus LayerDiffuse effect layers and procedural rain or snow. Cache likely event variants per layer in the background to hide latency.

## Drift benchmarks exist for flat editors only

Flat-editor drift is now well quantified. On EdiVal-Agent, content consistency falls about 3–4 points per turn for every editor, and Qwen-Image-Edit's overall score drops from 78 to 42 in three turns. Flow models also brighten progressively, and VLM judges miss quality decay that HPSv3 catches ([EdiVal-Agent](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.13399.md)). After ten add/remove turns in TailSteer, outside-box PSNR is **16.9 dB for Qwen-Image-2.1** and 18.6 dB for FLUX.1 Kontext dev ([TailSteer](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06741)). In MTEdit, Kontext-dev's consistency falls to 1.76/10 by turn 8 ([MTEdit](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23751)). On the layered side, LimeCross keeps RGBA across 2–3-layer sequential edits and reports infinite preservation PSNR, which its authors themselves call trivial. It never measures error growth against step count ([LimeCross](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10319)). ImageDirector's background SSIM of 0.998 versus 0.922 is the closest empirical support for layered-style compositing, but it covers only three edits ([ImageDirector](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830709)). **No paper measures decompose → recompose → re-decompose cycle error.**

The reusable tooling is ready. The EdiVal code accepts a custom editor class and computes GroundingDINO+DINOv3 consistency; its MIT/CC BY-SA licence split is unresolved ([EdiVal GitHub](https://github.com/tianyucodings/EdiVal)). ViStoryBench's character-identity (CIDS) and style evaluators are Apache-2.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/vistorybench/vistorybench)). Qwen-Image-Layered's protocol (alpha-weighted L1 and soft IoU) supplies per-layer metrics ([CVPR paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Yin_Qwen-Image-Layered_Towards_Inherent_Editability_via_Layer_Decomposition_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)).

**What to do.** Run 20 scenes × 10 turns, plus a 20-turn extension on 5 scenes, in three arms: layered (A), flat chained editor (B: Qwen-Image-2.1 or Kontext Q4), and layered with re-decomposition every turn (C, the novel arm). Use one Ollama-generated instruction CSV for all arms. Per turn, log outside-region PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, EdiVal-CC, masked DreamSim or CIDS, per-layer alpha IoU and L1, HPSv3, and mean and 99.9% luminance. Report AUC and the first turn where PSNR drops below 25 dB. Run a five-edit timing pilot first, because every T4 timing for the flat editors is extrapolated (roughly 60–120 s per 1024² Kontext edit [U]).

## ImageDirector: compositing removes drift, but not the lighting gap

Layer- and region-level generative editing has been studied mostly with experts. Layered Diffusion Brushes recruited artists (SUS 80 versus about 38 for the baselines) ([arXiv 2405.00313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00313)). LACE recruited professionals and is a GenAICHI workshop paper ([arXiv 2504.15189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15189v2)). SketchFlex targets novices but is region-based ([arXiv 2502.07556](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07556)). An unpublished 2023 Stanford thesis reports a novice layer study, with no details recoverable ([PURL](https://purl.stanford.edu/tm287xk2802)). DirectGPT set the "direct manipulation vs prompt-only" template: 50% faster, with 72% shorter prompts ([PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~dmasson/pdfs/directgpt.pdf)).

**ImageDirector** (UIST '26) is the closest work. It ran a within-subjects AB/BA study, N=12, with mostly AI novices. Position error fell from 207 to 37 px and perceived control rose from 4.39 to 5.89, but visual cohesion did not differ, and users converged on text for semantics plus direct manipulation for space ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830709)). It re-segments on every edit and has no story loop or agency measures. On the story side, 1001 Nights turns player keywords into visuals across 200+ players ([AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/27539)). Toyteller lets users steer narrative by moving symbols but generates no scene images ([arXiv 2501.13284](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13284v1)). Hidden Door *pre-generates* art rather than editing live ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/hidden-door-reveals-its-ai-powered-narrative-game-building-platform/)). Two measurement facts shape the design. "Who Did It?" links expectation match to sense of agency over generated images ([project](https://whodidit.mpi-inf.mpg.de/)). Players rate content they *believe* is AI-made as more frustrating ([arXiv 2602.14254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14254)), so disclosure must be identical across conditions. The German PXI is validated with N=506 ([MuC'22](https://www-live.dfki.de/fileadmin/user_upload/import/12535_MuC22__German_PXI_Version.pdf)).

**What this means for us.** We are not running a user study. ImageDirector is useful as technical evidence: compositing instead of regenerating removes background drift (SSIM 0.998 vs 0.922), but does not by itself fix lighting and cohesion. That is exactly what the prototype's re-harmonisation step (`relocate()` + IC-Light + shadow layer) has to address. Its masked-SSIM drift metric is reused in `drift_eval.py`.

## Updated gaps and research questions

| Gap | Status after this round | Change |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** Long-sequence drift, layered vs flat | **Open, sharper.** Flat drift is quantified (EdiVal, MTEdit, TailSteer, Omni IIE). LimeCross and ImageDirector give only trivial or three-step layered evidence. Cycle (re-decomposition) error is unmeasured anywhere | Strongest gap. Arm C is novel |
| **G2** Story events across layers | **Open, confirmed.** No layered global-edit method. StyleAligned and DreamLayer are mechanisms only | The four-arm strategy comparison is itself a contribution |
| **G3** Re-harmonise after move/resize | **Narrowed.** relocate() v5 plus IC-Light fbc plus procedural shadows is buildable training-free. ImageDirector shows experts still see a cohesion deficit | Shift from "can it be done" to "does it close the cohesion gap at interactive latency" |
| **G4** HCI of layer editing for novices/players | Context only: **not pursued** (no user study in this project) | n/a |
| **G5** Latency on free hardware | **Partly answered by us** (QIL 27–35 s, Stable-Layers 34 s, RevealLayer 69 s). Qwen-Image-2.1 on T4 not yet measured. No game-specific wait-tolerance study exists ([PSU Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/while-we-wait-how-users-perceive-waiting-times-and-generation-cue/)) | Fold into RQ3 as a logged covariate |

The research questions stand, with small adjustments. **RQ1** (do persistent layer stacks reduce drift?) should adopt the EdiVal harness and TailSteer metrics, add arm C, and use Qwen-Image-2.1 as the flat baseline, since its own 10-turn PSNR (16.9 dB) is already published. **RQ2** (event routing) becomes the four-arm comparison above. **RQ3** (user study) is dropped: the project is a prototype. **RQ4** (re-harmonisation) should measure against ImageDirector's finding that full regeneration matches compositing on cohesion. **RQ5** (latency frontier) remains a supporting result.

## Conclusion

Over this round, the bottleneck has moved. Six weeks ago, the question was whether any open layered model would run on a free T4. Now Qwen-Image-2.1, QIL, SDXL LayerDiffuse and IC-Light jointly cover every mechanical step, and the FLUX models are a debugging exercise rather than a blocker. Meanwhile the literature has built exactly the measuring instruments we need, from per-turn drift benchmarks to masked-SSIM user studies, and pointed them only at flat editors. The defensible contribution is therefore the prototype loop itself, plus a technical measurement of how persistent RGBA layers behave over long story sequences.

ImageDirector's cohesion result is the most important caveat. Layers will almost certainly win on preservation by construction. The real test is whether harmonisation and event routing can stop them losing on lighting and integration. The drift evaluation should be designed to detect that trade-off, not to assume it away.
